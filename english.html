A descent in seven stops

Belowthe 24thFloor

Every morning, I take the lift down from the 24th floor of my Shenzhen apartment. Every few levels it brakes hard. A few expressionless beasts of burden step in. The shove feels like a hybrid ride-hailing car, complete with the driver's hair-oil smell. Both make me carsick. I have not had breakfast and already want to throw up.

— the body resigns before the mind does.

Someone else's
better life.

On the office skybridge, grandparents herd toddlers in a dialect I cannot understand. It is not a park, but it has become theirs. I imagine they belong to an engineer next door—the kind paid a million yuan to miss dinner.

He works past midnight to give the family a better life. The better life waits downstairs.

Hong Kong is
one checkpoint
away.

One weekend I went to Hong Kong. Across the border, I suddenly wanted to dress properly again. Young people looked clean, exercised, awake. Of course, comparing a Hong Kong weekend with a Shenzhen workday is cheating.

On the way back, the checkpoint passed. The familiar exhaustion returned.
MEMORY

Bonsoir.

Hong Kong made me think of Paris. Dusk on the way home. A neighbour saying bonsoir in the stairwell. I was free then, and Paris did not have to pay my salary.

Paris was poetry and elsewhere. Shenzhen was going global and making money. I wanted both.
