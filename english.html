A descent in seven stops

Belowthe 24thFloor

Every morning, I take the lift down from the 24th floor of my Shenzhen apartment. Every few levels it brakes hard—the same shove as a hybrid ride-hailing car, complete with the driver's hair-oil smell. Both make me carsick. I have not had breakfast and already want to throw up.

— the body resigns before the mind does.
G / 08:17

Someone else's
better life.

On the office skybridge, grandparents herd toddlers in a dialect I cannot understand. It is not a park, but it has become theirs. I imagine they belong to an engineer next door—the kind paid a million yuan to miss dinner.

He works past midnight to give the family a better life. The better life waits downstairs.

CROCS

First, lipstick.
Then, Crocs.

On day one, I dressed as if Beijing CBD might be watching. Proper trousers. Make-up. Intentions. Within a week, I was wearing Crocs. Shenzhen had defeated me through footwear.

Adaptation can look exactly like surrender. Sometimes it is simply Tuesday.

Hong Kong is
one checkpoint
away.

One weekend I went to Hong Kong. Across the border, I suddenly wanted to dress properly again. Young people looked clean, exercised, awake. Of course, comparing a Hong Kong weekend with a Shenzhen workday is cheating.

On the way back, the checkpoint passed. The familiar exhaustion returned.

At eight one evening, I took the lift down to throw away the rubbish. A woman beside me answered her boss: “I'm outside. Can it wait?”

The lift kept going down.

At 11 p.m., the men on the metro resemble a match-three game: glasses, phones, soft middles, the faint shine of overtime. I am there in Crocs, holding my phone, wearing the same face. Match any three to clear the level.

Perhaps they, like me, are online complaining about the opposite sex.

MEMORY

Bonsoir.

Coming back from Hong Kong made me think of Paris. Dusk in the stairwell. A neighbour saying bonsoir. I was studying then; Paris did not have to pay my salary. The comparison is cheating.

Still, I had not noticed the sky in a while.
